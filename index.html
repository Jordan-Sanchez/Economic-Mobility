Core Empirical Drivers & Policy Insights

Synthesizing findings from Chetty, Hendren, Kline, Saez & Opportunity Insights

Growth vs. Distribution

In the 1940s, rapid GDP growth was paired with broad income sharing across all quintiles. Since 1980, productivity gains concentrated heavily in the top quintile, shrinking upward mobility for lower income groups.

Geographic Geography

Mobility varies dramatically by commuting zone. Cities with lower spatial segregation, higher social capital, better public schools, and stronger family structure exhibit upwards of 2x higher upward mobility.

Higher Ed Colleges

Colleges serve as mobility engines. Mid-tier public universities (e.g., CUNY, Cal State) have the highest "Mobility Rates" by taking large percentages of low-income students and elevating them into the top quintile.

Economic Connectedness

Cross-class social ties ("Economic Connectedness") are among the strongest predictors of upward mobility. Low-income children who grow up in neighborhoods with high cross-class interaction experience higher earnings.
